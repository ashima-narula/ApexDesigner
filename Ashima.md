# Intentional Criminal Intent in Fragmented Healthcare Systems

## 1. Introduction
- Intentional criminal intent refers to deliberate misuse of healthcare data  
- Includes:
  - Data theft  
  - Unauthorised access  
  - Data manipulation  
  - System disruption  
- Actors:
  - Insiders (employees, contractors)  
  - External attackers  
  - Cybercriminal groups  
- Insider threats are critical due to existing access  

---

## 2. Understanding Criminal Intent
- Malicious insider = misuse of authorised access  
- Examples:
  - Accessing patient records without reason  
  - Sharing confidential data  
  - Credential misuse  
  - Assisting external attackers  
- Insider threats are a major cause of breaches  

---

## 3. Why Fragmentation Increases Risk
- Data stored across:
  - Multiple databases  
  - Different systems  
  - Disconnected platforms  
- Issues:
  - Poor monitoring  
  - Inconsistent security  
  - Weak access control  
- Leads to:
  - Increased attack surface  
  - Reduced accountability  

---

## 4. Types of Criminal Activities
- **Insider misuse**
  - Unauthorised access  
- **Credential abuse**
  - Sharing login details  
- **Financial cybercrime**
  - Identity theft  
  - Insurance fraud  
- **Ransomware / sabotage**
  - System disruption  
- Common threats:
  - Phishing  
  - Malware  
  - Hackers  

---

## 5. Research Evidence
- Breaches involve:
  - Human behaviour  
  - Organisational weaknesses  
- Findings:
  - Insider misuse is common  
  - Weak governance increases risk  
  - Poor system design enables attacks  
- Responses:
  - Termination  
  - Legal action  
  - Training  

---

## 6. Impact on Patients
- Privacy violations  
- Identity theft  
- Financial fraud  
- Exposure of medical data  
- Delayed treatment  

---

## 7. Impact on Organisations
- Financial losses  
- Legal penalties  
- Operational shutdown  
- Reputational damage  
- Loss of trust  
- Cybersecurity = patient safety issue  

---

## 8. Role of Fragmentation in Crime
- Makes attacks easier:
  - Incomplete logs  
  - Different security standards  
  - Inconsistent permissions  
- Results:
  - Duplicate records  
  - Over-access  
  - Hidden misuse  
- Attackers can move across systems undetected  

---

## 9. Risk Reduction Strategies

### Technical Controls
- Role-based access control  
- Least privilege  
- Multi-factor authentication  
- Encryption  
- Audit logs  

### Organisational Controls
- Staff training  
- Behaviour monitoring  
- Vendor management  
- Risk assessments  
- Quick account deactivation  

---

## 10. Conclusion
- Intentional criminal intent is a major healthcare risk  
- Fragmentation increases vulnerability  
- Problem = weak systems + misuse  
- Solutions:
  - Better integration  
  - Strong security policies  
  - Continuous monitoring  
- Goal: Protect patient data and ensure safety
